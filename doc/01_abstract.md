## Abstract

This project studies whether a flow-inspired training idea can improve neural collaborative filtering on a smaller MovieLens benchmark. We compare four recommender families under a unified training and evaluation setup: `ItemCF`, `BiasedMF`, `ClassicNeuMF`, and `FlowNeuMF`. The first three serve as progressively stronger baselines, while `FlowNeuMF` introduces a flow-style regularization strategy designed to reshape the optimization trajectory of the neural ranker instead of changing the core recommendation task itself.

The main research question is simple: if the baseline neural recommender already predicts user-item preference from embeddings and multilayer interactions, does adding a flow-motivated objective make top-N ranking behave better? To answer that, the project tracks both rating prediction metrics and ranking metrics. We report `MAE` and `RMSE` for pointwise accuracy, together with `Precision@10`, `Recall@10`, `F-measure@10`, and `NDCG@10` for recommendation quality.

The final experiments show a mixed but useful outcome. `ItemCF` remains unusually strong on rating error for this reduced dataset, while `BiasedMF` is the strongest classic baseline on ranking quality. More importantly for the project hypothesis, `FlowNeuMF` consistently improves over `ClassicNeuMF` on all reported ranking metrics while keeping nearly identical `RMSE`. That makes the flow-based variant a credible direction for further work, even though it does not yet beat every traditional baseline.
